## Deviations from the plan, with reasons

1. `donor_corpus(candidate, parent, trajectories, workers)` replaces the plan's `(edits, run_trajectories, worker, *,
   bench)`. The edits come from `package.edits`, the bench from the trajectories, and there is one worker per
   (package, donor item), like `router.plan`.
2. sham_compute, sham_repair and the shadow trigger are the shell's (A6, ticket A6-1). `placebo.sham_discourage` IS
   `solver.sham_discouraged`, and there is no `placebo.shadow`. T-PLACEBO-4/5 drive them through `solver.solve`
   with A9's sealed content.
3. Band targets: for `before_call`/`bindings`, the real note's rendered length on the acceptance item (zero calls,
   public view, lengths only). For `sheet_feedback` and ALFWorld steps, whose real note exists only after dispatch,
   the median donor note length. A clip drops trailing entries and then cuts the last text entry. An extension uses
   the same donor's next output or steps. Neither goes beyond the procedure's `max_note_chars`.
4. P_i holds `facts` only: no bindings, so no `<calc>` invitation. It is length-matched to the P_y sham and runs on
   SB, OfficeQA and LiveMath only (owner #9 default).
5. The harmful or helpful sham rule uses a donor-clustered percentile bootstrap (`stats_ext.bootstrap_ci` with
   clusters = donor) of P_y − S on the analysed A* items. A harmful sham sets J2 to not informative, judges J1 at
   0.025 and uses the registered wording.
6. A J5 failure (infra > 10 %, reasoning mismatch > 1 %, shared unanswered > 10 % of A*, or median start spread
   > 5 %) makes the round `undetermined`, with `pause` set for infra or reasoning. Missing timestamps fail the
   balance. A J6 failure (two-sided p < 0.01) is `undetermined` and "the run pauses".
7. The seeded order is blocked by (set, item, run) unit, with the arm order seeded per unit, not a uniform shuffle.
   A uniform shuffle fails the registered 5 % start-balance guard by chance in 86-99 % of rounds with 30-200 cells
   per arm (ticket A9-2; owner confirmation requested).
8. `dispatch(..., items=)` takes the registered item records, because the registry hashes records. A too-small round
   also releases the probe items.
9. `cell_arm` also returns the cell's `arm_kind` (plan §2.8 / `cells.ARM_KINDS`).
10. `prepare` takes `generate`, `probe`, `freshness` and `mde` from the caller; `protocol.py` has no MDE constant
    (ticket A9-3). G_acc is checked: at most one generated item per A* item, and at most `protocol.G_ACC[bench]`.
11. `decision.json` has every key of plan §2.6 plus `chain`, `unit`, `analysed`, `shared_unanswered`,
    `repair_call_rates`, `sealed.record` and `sealed.donor_corpus`, and J1/J2 guard entries. `J5.balance` is
    `{"ok", "spread_s", "duration_s"}` (the plan shows a bool). J6 names its pair first − second (`C-S`/`S_dup-S`)
    with `ok`. A cell without a judge (a stopped cell) is refused by `decide`.
12. J7 is evaluated whenever probe items were sealed; the caller passes them on LiveMath's formal chain only.
13. The acceptance tests patch sign flips and bootstrap resamples to 2,000 and 400 for speed. Both are sensitivity
    or interval figures, and T-STATS covers the registered counts. The validity tests use the registered 10,000
    permutations with seed 20261021.
