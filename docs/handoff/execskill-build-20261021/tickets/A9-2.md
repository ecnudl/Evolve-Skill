# Ticket A9-2 → A8 (`rounds.py`), main agent / owner: the J5 start-balance guard fails by chance under a uniform shuffle

From A9 (`acceptance.py`), 2026-10-22. Registration §6.2 (ME-F28), §6.4 J5; plan rows `rounds.py` and `acceptance.py`.

Registration §6.2 puts every cell of a round into one seeded order, never sorted by arm. It cites cal-1's practice,
the uniform shuffle of `coevo5/step.py:105-121` (`_order` at :79-80). It also makes the round "undetermined" when
the arms' median dispatch start times differ by more than 5 % of the round's duration.

Under a uniform shuffle, that guard fails by chance in most realistic rounds. A thread pool keeps the ratio of
start-time to position. I simulated 1,000 shuffles per row (arms = the non-twin arms; m = cells per arm):

| round | arms | m | P(spread > 5 %) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALFWorld, A* = 30, 1 run | 4 | 30 | 0.98 |
| SpreadsheetBench, A* = 30, 2 runs | 5 | 60 | 0.99 |
| SpreadsheetBench, A* = 60, 2 runs | 5 | 120 | 0.95 |
| OfficeQA, A* = 45, 4 runs | 5 | 180 | 0.87 |
| LiveMath, A* = 100, 2 runs | 5 | 200 | 0.86 |
| SearchQA, A* = 300 | 4 | 300 | 0.62 |
| SearchQA, A* = 1000 | 4 | 1000 | 0.09 |

## What A9 did

`acceptance.schedule` (`_ordered`) is still one seeded order per block and never sorted by arm. It is blocked by unit:

- the (set, item, run) units are in the sha256 order of (SEED, `round_tag(unit)`:block, unit);
- the arms of each unit are in the sha256 order of (SEED, tag, unit, arm).

So every arm moves through the block at the same pace. The median spread is then at most a few positions, and the
guard catches real delays, such as an arm's cells waiting on a sandbox or slot. Tested in
`tests/test_execskill_acceptance.py::test_t_acc_2_one_seeded_order_never_by_arm_with_every_arm_at_the_same_pace`.
