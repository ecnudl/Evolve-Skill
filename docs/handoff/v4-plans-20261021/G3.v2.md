### 0.3 The design in brief

1. **One hit list, one record (B3, B4).** `leakage.hits(text, corpus)` returns every mechanical hit in `REASONS`
   order:
   - shared 8-gram runs (overlapping shared 8-grams merge into one hit);
   - then `answer_text`, `answer_number`, `option_text` and `answer_word`.

   Each hit carries:
   - `snippet`: the matched text as the checks read it, at most 80 characters;
   - `item`: the training item it comes from (an 8-gram: its first holder in pool order; an answer: its first holder
     in v2's seeded order);
   - `task`: what the adjudicator is shown of that item (its question, or its input around the 8-gram), at most 600
     characters;
   - `template` and `hard`;
   - for answers only: `answer` and `items`.

   `leakage.entries` turns hits into B3's record. `leakage.mechanical` stays as a descriptive wrapper (the first hit's
   kind), so `smoke.paths` is unchanged.
2. **The one mechanical drop (B4): a hard hit.** A hit is hard when all of the following hold:
   - the rule contains a reference answer verbatim, as v1's matching reads it (the stricter matching kept from today):
     a whole-word run of the normalized text, a number by its value, or an option's content as a substring without
     whitespace;
   - the answer has at least 12 characters, counted on the normalized answer (a number: its digits and point, as
     the reference has it and, v2, as the rule writes it, thousands separators removed; an option: its content
     without whitespace);
   - it is not a single alphabetic word. The program has no dictionary, so a single alphabetic word is taken for a
     dictionary word;
   - it is not template text. Template text is an answer that recurs in the task inputs of at least 5% of the bench's
     items. LiveMath's meta-option recurs in 129 of the 283 training items (the comment at protocol.py line 220),
     so it is never hard (C1).

   A shared 8-gram is never hard.
3. **Before research (§4 step 5b, v2 §7 extended).**
   - A rule with a hard hit is `dropped_pre_dispatch`.
   - A rule with any other hit is kept (`"research": "skipped_hits"`) and sends no research request.
   - A research question with any hit is not sent (`not_dispatched`).

   So the researcher never sees a training item's content through a rule or a question.

   A research rewrite of a rule made of parts revises only its `do`/`otherwise` (`research.rewrite_parts`). The
   condition stays, and the code renders the text again. This is G2's contract C-4 (§4 (g), §4A).

   **v2, after the self-test (§4 (h)).** A similar candidate whose final text has any hit is not sent forward. The
   forward material requests and the generator would otherwise see the text. Its `similar_status` is
   `forward_not_dispatched_hits` and it stays at its own placement.
4. **Adjudication (B6, P4, C1).** Every rule text that reaches admission, and every self-test revision, is adjudicated
   once, by ROLE_VOTES = 3 sampled calls at temperature 0.7. Each call sees:
   - the bench's public task card;
   - the rule's text, plus its `when`/`do`/`otherwise` when the text is their rendering (P1);
   - its hits: at most 3 per kind, with the number left out.

   The labels are leak, illustrative and task_structure. The majority label decides; with no majority the label is
   leak. illustrative keeps the rule; task_structure keeps it while `LEAKAGE_ALLOW_TASK_STRUCTURE`.

   There is one label per rule text, judged over all its hits. v2 copies it into each of the text's admission or
   revision entries as `adjudicated_label`. The spec's "labels each hit" is open question 1, and B4 is
   `needs_decision` on it.
5. **Statuses.**
   - `leak_mechanical`: a hard hit at admission.
   - `leak_adjudicated`: the label is leak.
   - `leak_task_structure`: the label is task_structure and the switch is off.
   - `dropped_pre_dispatch`: as before.
   - Self-test revisions use `"revision_" +` the same names.
   - Retired: `leak_adjudicated_word`, and the research mark `skipped_answer_word` (now `skipped_hits`). No module
     outside `step.py` reads either string (checked with grep over `skillopt/`, `scripts/` and `tests/`).
6. **Forward checker (P2, P4).**
   - Three sampled check calls per item, each writing its reasoning first and each prompt ending with an imaginary
     worked example.
   - A sample's outcome:
     - OfficeQA style: `agrees`, `check_literal_not_in_table` or `check_mismatch`;
     - LiveMath style: `agrees` or `check_rejected`.
   - The majority outcome decides; with no majority the item is `check_invalid`.
   - The vote record is kept in the round's item row.
7. **B12.** A LiveMath option's content is in the answer table only with at least 2 words or at least 10 characters,
   on top of v1's 4-character floor.

### 1.1 G4: role calls (G4.md §1.2 and §3.2, final)

- **`roles.Roles.vote(role, system, user, *, label, check, label_of, tie, votes=None) -> (label, record)`.**
  - It runs `Roles.ask` ROLE_VOTES (= 3) times, at labels `<label>/v1`, `<label>/v2` and `<label>/v3`, at
    `ROLE_VOTE_TEMPERATURE` (= 0.7).
  - `check` validates each sample, with B5's re-ask. The parse step is the identity, and `label_of(parsed)` gives
    the sample's label.
  - The majority means more than half of ROLE_VOTES. An invalid or undelivered sample has no label.
  - `record = {"label", "majority", "tally": {label: n}, "invalid", "votes": [{"label", "reason", "valid"}, ...]}`,
    where each `"reason"` is `parsed.get("reason")`.
  - G3 archives the record whole and reads `record["tally"]` only in tests.
- **`roles.reasoned(key, allowed, *, limit=None)`.** The validator of `{"reason", key}`.
  - Both keys are required and other keys are tolerated.
  - The reason is clipped to `ROLE_REASON_CHARS` (= 300).
  - It returns `{"reason", key}`, and its message is `"<key> must be one of: <sorted allowed>"`.
- **The other `roles.py` helpers.**
  - `roles.clipped(value, limit, *, name=)`.
  - `roles.fields(value, required)`: extra keys are tolerated; the message is `"missing field(s): ..."`.
  - `roles.text(value, *, limit, name=)`.
  - `roles.Invalid`.
  - `roles.problem`: it clips a validator's message to `PROBLEM_CHARS` = 300. G3's longest message is the formula
    re-ask in §5 (d): 111 characters plus the grammar error's name (126 with `formula_element`).
- **B5 and B17 in `Roles.ask`.**
  - B5: one re-ask whose payload is `{**user, "notice": "Your previous reply failed validation: <message>. Reply with

- **`protocol.py`.** `ROLE_VOTES = 3`, `ROLE_VOTE_TEMPERATURE = 0.7`, `ROLE_REASON_CHARS = 300`. Also

```python
def normalize(text):
    """NFKC, lower case, every punctuation or symbol character a space, whitespace collapsed."""
    require(type(text) is str, "Only text is normalized")
    text = unicodedata.normalize("NFKC", text).lower()
    return " ".join("".join(" " if unicodedata.category(ch)[0] in "PS" else ch for ch in text).split())


def surface(benchmark, item, *, task_text=None):
    """The item's task surface: its question with the supplied context (SearchQA: the snippets, one per line; OfficeQA:
    the pages) or with the options (LiveMath), SpreadsheetBench's instruction, or ALFWorld's ``task_text`` (the
    episode's initial observation from this step's training execution)."""
    public = item["public"]
    if benchmark in ("searchqa", "officeqa"):
        context = public["context"]
        if type(context) is list:
            require(all(type(c) is str for c in context), "A context is text")
            context = "\n".join(context)
        require(type(public["question"]) is str and type(context) is str, "A QA item is text")
        value = public["question"] + "\n" + context
    elif benchmark == "livemath":
        require(type(public["question"]) is str and type(public["choices"]) is str, "A LiveMath item is text")
        value = public["question"] + "\n" + public["choices"]
    elif benchmark == "spreadsheetbench":
        value = public["instruction"]
    elif benchmark == "alfworld":
        value = task_text
    else:
        raise ValueError("Unknown benchmark")
    require(type(value) is str, "An item's surface is text")
    return value


def _grams(tokens):
    return {tuple(tokens[i:i + LEAK_NGRAM]) for i in range(len(tokens) - LEAK_NGRAM + 1)}


def _significant(number):
    """Significant digits of a decimal as written: its digits without sign, point and leading zeros."""
    return len(number.lstrip("+-").replace(".", "").lstrip("0"))


def _value(written):
    """The exact absolute value of a number as written (separators removed); None beyond the exponent bound."""
    number = Decimal(written.replace(",", ""))
    return abs(Fraction(number)) if abs(number.adjusted()) <= MAX_EXPONENT else None


def _numeric(answer):
    """The reference's value when it is a number once ",", "$", "%" and surrounding space are removed, else None."""
    cleaned = answer.replace(",", "").replace("$", "").replace("%", "").strip()
    return cleaned if REFERENCE_NUMBER.fullmatch(cleaned) else None


def _seeded(task_id):
    """v2 §7: the seeded order in which the training items holding the same answer are shown."""
    return hashlib.sha256(json.dumps([DATA_SEED, "leak", task_id]).encode()).hexdigest()


def _hold(table, key, item, answer, **extra):
    """Record ``item`` (once) as a holder of ``key`` in ``table``: {"item", "question", "answer": as written}."""
    holders = table.setdefault(key, [])
    if all(holder["item"] != item["task_id"] for holder in holders):
        holders.append({"item": item["task_id"], "question": item["public"]["question"], "answer": answer, **extra})


class Corpus:
    """What the checks compare a text with: one bench's training pool (``items``: pool items of that bench; ALFWorld:
    ``alfworld_texts`` {task_id: initial observation}, an item without one is skipped). v4 B3/B4: it keeps where every
    hit comes from -- ``grams``: each non-template 8-gram and the first item (pool order) holding it; ``phrases``,
    ``words``, ``numbers``, ``options``: each reference answer and its holders, {"item", "question", "answer" as
    written} -- and the items' normalized inputs (``inputs``, for the context of an 8-gram and the template test of an
    answer, ``recurring``)."""

    def __init__(self, benchmark, items, *, alfworld_texts=None):
        require(benchmark in ORDER, "Unknown benchmark")
        require(all(item["benchmark"] == benchmark for item in items), "A corpus holds one bench's items")
        texts = alfworld_texts or {}
        surfaces = []  # (task id, normalized surface), pool order
        for item in items:
            if benchmark == "alfworld":
                if item["task_id"] in texts:
                    surfaces.append((item["task_id"], normalize(surface(benchmark, item,
                                                                        task_text=texts[item["task_id"]]))))
            else:
                surfaces.append((item["task_id"], normalize(surface(benchmark, item))))
        grams = [(task_id, _grams(value.split())) for task_id, value in surfaces]
        counts = Counter(gram for _, found in grams for gram in found)
        self.benchmark, self.size = benchmark, len(grams)
        self.template = frozenset(g for g, c in counts.items() if Fraction(c, len(grams)) >= LEAK_TEMPLATE_SHARE)
        self.grams = {}
        for task_id, found in grams:
            for gram in found - self.template:
                self.grams.setdefault(gram, task_id)
        self.inputs = {task_id: " %s " % value for task_id, value in surfaces}  # padded for whole-word tests
        self._squeezed = ["".join(value.split()) for _, value in surfaces]
        self._recurring = {}
        self.phrases, self.words, self.numbers, self.options = {}, {}, {}, {}
        for item in items:
            if benchmark == "searchqa":
                answers = item["private"]["answers"]
                require(type(answers) is list and all(type(a) is str for a in answers), "SearchQA answers are text")
                for answer in answers:
                    self._text_answer(answer, item)
            elif benchmark == "officeqa":
                answer = item["private"]["answer"]
                require(type(answer) is str, "An OfficeQA answer is text")
                value = _numeric(answer)
                if value is None:
                    self._text_answer(answer, item)
                elif _significant(value) >= LEAK_MIN_SIGNIFICANT_DIGITS:
                    _hold(self.numbers, _value(value), item, answer, digits=value.lstrip("+-"))
            elif benchmark == "livemath":
                options, letter = livemath_options(item["public"]["choices"]), item["private"]["answer"]
                require(options is not None and type(letter) is str and len(letter) == 1 and letter in LIVEMATH_LABELS,
                        "A LiveMath item has five options and a letter")
                written = options[LIVEMATH_LABELS.index(letter)]
                words = normalize(written)
                content = "".join(words.split())
                if len(content) >= LEAK_MIN_OPTION_CHARS and (len(words.split()) >= LEAK_OPTION_WORDS
                                                             or len(content) >= LEAK_OPTION_CHARS):  # v4 B12
                    _hold(self.options, content, item, written, words=words)
        self._starts = {}  # a multi-word answer's first word -> its phrases (the search of ``hits``)
        for phrase in self.phrases:
            self._starts.setdefault(phrase[0], []).append(phrase)

    def _text_answer(self, answer, item):
        tokens = tuple(normalize(answer).split())
        if len(tokens) >= 2:
            _hold(self.phrases, tokens, item, answer)
        elif len(tokens) == 1 and len(tokens[0]) >= LEAK_MIN_SINGLE_WORD:
            _hold(self.words, tokens[0], item, answer)

    def recurring(self, text, *, squeezed=False):
        """v4 B4/C1: whether ``text`` (normalized) recurs in the task inputs of at least LEAK_TEMPLATE_SHARE of the
        items -- as a whole-word run, or with ``squeezed`` as a substring of the inputs without whitespace: template
        wording of the bench (LiveMath's meta-option among the options), never a hard hit. Memoized."""
        key = (text, squeezed)
        if key not in self._recurring:
            if squeezed:
                found = sum(text in value for value in self._squeezed)
            else:
                needle = " %s " % text
                found = sum(needle in value for value in self.inputs.values())
            self._recurring[key] = self.size > 0 and Fraction(found, self.size) >= LEAK_TEMPLATE_SHARE
        return self._recurring[key]

    def window(self, task_id, words):
        """v4 B4: the item's normalized input around ``words`` (an 8-gram it holds), at most LEAK_CONTEXT_CHARS
        characters."""
        value = self.inputs[task_id]
        start = max(0, value.find(" %s " % words) - (LEAK_CONTEXT_CHARS - len(words)) // 2)
        return value[start:start + LEAK_CONTEXT_CHARS].strip()


def _numbers(text):
    """(position, the number as written, its absolute value) of each number written in ``text`` (a fraction p/q also
    as its value), in order of position, except four-digit years 1900-2030."""
    text = unicodedata.normalize("NFKC", text)
    found = []
    for match in NUMBER.finditer(text):
        raw = match.group()
        if YEAR.fullmatch(raw) and YEARS[0] <= int(raw) <= YEARS[1]:
            continue
        value = _value(raw)
        if value is not None:
            found.append((match.start(), raw, value))
    for match in FRACTION.finditer(text):
        if int(match.group(2)):
            found.append((match.start(), match.group(), Fraction(int(match.group(1)), int(match.group(2)))))
    return sorted(found, key=lambda entry: entry[:2])


def _single_word(text):
    """v4 B4: a single alphabetic word, taken for a dictionary word (the program has no dictionary)."""
    return " " not in text and text.isalpha()


def _answer(kind, snippet, holders, *, template, long):
    """One answer hit: its item is the first holder in the seeded order (v2 §7); hard (v4 B4) when ``long`` and not
    template text."""
    first = min(holders, key=lambda holder: _seeded(holder["item"]))
    return {"hit": kind, "snippet": snippet[:LEAK_SNIPPET_CHARS], "item": first["item"], "items": len(holders),
            "template": template, "hard": long and not template, "task": first["question"][:LEAK_CONTEXT_CHARS],
            "answer": first["answer"]}

    (normalized; a number as written) clipped to LEAK_SNIPPET_CHARS, "item": the training item it comes from (an
    8-gram's first holder in pool order; an answer's first holder in the seeded order), "template", "hard" (the one
    mechanical drop, module docstring), "task": what the adjudicator is shown of that item (its question, or its input

    "answer" (the item's reference as written)."""
    require(type(text) is str and isinstance(corpus, Corpus), "A text and a corpus")

    for i in range(len(tokens) - LEAK_NGRAM + 1):

        if holder is None:
            continue

            runs[-1][1] = i + LEAK_NGRAM
        else:
            runs.append([i, i + LEAK_NGRAM, holder])
    for start, end, holder in runs:
        out.append({"hit": "shared_8gram", "snippet": " ".join(tokens[start:end])[:LEAK_SNIPPET_CHARS],
                    "item": holder, "template": False, "hard": False,
                    "task": corpus.window(holder, " ".join(tokens[start:start + LEAK_NGRAM]))})
    phrases = {}
    for i, token in enumerate(tokens):
        for phrase in corpus._starts.get(token, ()):
            if phrase not in phrases and tuple(tokens[i:i + len(phrase)]) == phrase:
                phrases[phrase] = i
    for phrase in sorted(phrases, key=lambda p: (phrases[p], p)):
        content = " ".join(phrase)
        out.append(_answer("answer_text", content, corpus.phrases[phrase], template=corpus.recurring(content),
                           long=len(content) >= LEAK_HARD_DROP_CHARS))
    numbers = {}
    for _, written, value in (_numbers(text) if corpus.numbers else ()):
        if value in corpus.numbers:
            numbers.setdefault(value, written)
    for value, written in numbers.items():
        holders = corpus.numbers[value]
        out.append(_answer("answer_number", written, holders, template=False,
                           long=len(written.replace(",", "")) >= LEAK_HARD_DROP_CHARS
                           and max(len(h["digits"]) for h in holders) >= LEAK_HARD_DROP_CHARS))
    squeezed = "".join(tokens)
    for _, content in sorted((squeezed.find(c), c) for c in corpus.options if c in squeezed):
        holders = corpus.options[content]
        words = holders[0]["words"]
        out.append(_answer("option_text", words, holders, template=corpus.recurring(content, squeezed=True),
                           long=len(content) >= LEAK_HARD_DROP_CHARS and not _single_word(words)))
    for word in dict.fromkeys(token for token in tokens if token in corpus.words):
        out.append(_answer("answer_word", word, corpus.words[word], template=corpus.recurring(word),
                           long=len(word) >= LEAK_HARD_DROP_CHARS and not _single_word(word)))

def mechanical(text, corpus):
    """None when ``text`` (a rule, or a research question) passes every mechanical check of ``corpus``'s bench, else
    the first hit's kind in ``REASONS`` order -- "answer_word" only when it is the only kind. Descriptive since v4 B4
    (the smoke's paths unit decides with it what may be sent); the stage reads ``hits``."""
    found = hits(text, corpus)

def entries(rule_id, check, found, *, question=None):
    """v4 B3: the record of the mechanical decision on one text -- one entry per hit of ``found`` (``hits``): {"rule",
    "check" (one of ``CHECKS``), "question" (the research question's number, for a question), "hit", "snippet",
    "item", "hard", "decision"}. The decision is "dropped" for every hit of a research question and of a similar
    candidate's forward dispatch (neither is sent, v4 B4) and of a rule text with a hard hit (the rule is dropped
    without adjudication), else "adjudicate"."""
    require(check in CHECKS and (question is None) == (check != "question"), "A leakage check")
    dropped = question is not None or check == "forward" or any(hit["hard"] for hit in found)
    return [{"rule": rule_id, "check": check, **({} if question is None else {"question": question}),
             "hit": hit["hit"], "snippet": hit["snippet"], "item": hit["item"], "hard": hit["hard"],
             "decision": "dropped" if dropped else "adjudicate"} for hit in found]


def answer_values(answers, limit=60):
    """v3 §6: the distinct expected answer-cell values (``sheetview``) of SpreadsheetBench answers, in their order, at
    most ``limit``: blanks and booleans left out, numbers as written by Python."""
    out = []
    for answer in answers:
        for cell in ((answer.get("sheet_cells") or {}).get("shown") or []):
            value = cell.get("expected")
            if value is None or isinstance(value, bool) or (isinstance(value, str) and not value.strip()):
                continue
            value = str(value)
            if value not in out:
                out.append(value)
            if len(out) >= limit:
                return out
    return out


def shown(found):
    """v4 B4: the hits the adjudicator sees -- at most LEAK_SHOWN_PER_KIND of each kind, in order, each {"hit",
    "snippet", "task", "template"} and, for an answer, "answer" and "items" -- and how many are left out."""
    seen, out = Counter(), []
    for hit in found:
        seen[hit["hit"]] += 1
        if seen[hit["hit"]] <= LEAK_SHOWN_PER_KIND:
            out.append({key: hit[key] for key in ("hit", "snippet", "task", "answer", "items", "template")
                        if key in hit})
    return out, len(found) - len(out)


def adjudicate(roles, text, *, card, label, parts=None, hits=(), values=None):
    """The adjudicator's decision on one rule text (§9; every text that passed the mechanical drop). v4 B6: ``card``,
    the bench's public task card (what the solver sees); ``parts``, the rule's {"when", "do", "otherwise"} (P1) when
    the text is made of them; B4: ``hits`` (``hits``), shown with their context (``shown``); v3 §6: ``values``,
    SpreadsheetBench's expected answer-cell values of the shown failures (``answer_values``). P4: ROLE_VOTES sampled
    votes (``Roles.vote``), each a reason then one of LEAK_LABELS (B5: a refused reply is asked again once); the
    majority label decides, a tie (an invalid or undelivered vote included) is "leak". C1: kept when "illustrative",
    or "task_structure" while LEAKAGE_ALLOW_TASK_STRUCTURE. Returns {"label", "kept", "votes": the vote record (every
    sample's label and reason, the tally)}."""
    require(type(text) is str and text.strip(), "A rule to adjudicate")
    require(type(card) is str and card.strip(), "The bench's public task card")
    rule = {"text": text, **{key: value for key, value in (parts or {}).items()
                             if key in ("when", "do", "otherwise") and value}}
    message = {"task_card": card, "rule": rule}
    message["hits"], omitted = shown(list(hits))
    if omitted:
        message["hits_omitted"] = omitted
    if values:
        message["answer_values"] = list(values)
    decided, votes = roles.vote("leak_adjudicate", ADJUDICATE_SYSTEM, message, label=label,
                                check=reasoned("label", LEAK_LABELS), label_of=lambda value: value["label"],
                                tie="leak")
    kept = decided == "illustrative" or (decided == "task_structure" and LEAKAGE_ALLOW_TASK_STRUCTURE)
    return {"label": decided, "kept": kept, "votes": votes}
```

## 8. Registration-delta sentences (for the main session's v4 delta document)

- **B3.** Every mechanical leakage decision is recorded. Each hit on a rule's text, or on one of its research
  questions, is an entry:
  - `rule`;
  - `check`: pre_dispatch, question, admission, revision or (v2) forward;
  - `question`: the question's number, for a research question;
  - `hit`: its kind;
  - `snippet`: the matched text as the checks read it, at most 80 characters;
  - `item`: the training item it comes from;
  - `hard`;
  - `decision`: adjudicate or dropped.

  An adjudicated text's entries also carry `adjudicated_label`: the rule's one label, judged over all its hits.

  The entries are kept in four places: in `research.json` (pre-dispatch and question checks), in the rule's record
  (pre-dispatch and admission), in the prepare result's `leakage` list (every decision of the step) and, for a
  self-test revision or a withheld forward dispatch, in the self-test record.
- **B4.** Every hit kind goes to the adjudicator with its context. The kinds are shared 8-gram, answer text, answer
  word, answer number and option text. The context is the training item's question, or its input around a shared
  8-gram (at most 600 characters), plus the reference answer and how many training items hold it. The adjudicator is
  shown at most three hits of each kind, and the number left out is stated.

  This amends v2 §7. For a single-word answer, v3 showed up to three colliding training items. v4 shows one item per
  hit (its first holder in v2's seeded order) with the number of holders, and at most three hits per kind.

  The adjudication gives one label per rule text, judged over all its hits; a leak on any count makes the rule a
  leak. The spec's reading, one label per hit, is an owner decision (B4 is `needs_decision`).

  The one mechanical drop left is a hard hit. A hard hit is a reference answer contained verbatim, by v1's matching
  (a whole-word run of the normalized text, a number by its value, an option's content as a substring without
  whitespace), such that:
  - it has at least 12 characters (counted on the normalized answer; a number on its digits and point, both as the
    reference has it and as the rule writes it, thousands separators removed; an option on its content without
    whitespace);
  - it is not a single alphabetic word, which is taken for a dictionary word (the program has no dictionary);
  - it is not template text, meaning an answer that recurs in the inputs of at least 5% of the bench's items, such
    as LiveMath's meta-option.

  What happens to a rule:
  - With a hard hit, it is dropped before research (`dropped_pre_dispatch`) or at admission (`leak_mechanical`).
  - With any other hit, it is kept for the adjudicator but sends no research request (`skipped_hits`).
  - A research question with a hit is not sent.
  - A similar candidate whose final text has any hit is not sent forward (`forward_not_dispatched_hits`). So the
    researcher and the forward generator never see a text with a hit, as research.py's invariant requires.

  The v3 status `leak_adjudicated_word` and the research mark `skipped_answer_word` are retired.

  Owner decisions, recorded as such: the template exemption, the digits-and-point counting, and approximating "not a
  dictionary word" by "not a single alphabetic word" (open questions 3 and 4).
- **B6.** The adjudicator's input has three parts:
  - the bench's public task card, as the roles read it;
  - the rule's text and, when the text is their rendering, its condition, action and alternative (P1);
  - its hits.

  On SpreadsheetBench it also sees the expected answer-cell values, as in v3. It replies `{"reason", "label"}`,
  reason first, in three sampled votes. The majority label decides, and a tie is "leak". An undelivered or invalid
  vote counts toward no label.
- **B12.** The content of LiveMath's correct option is checked only when it has at least 2 words or at least 10
  characters. Both are counted on the normalized option, where symbols split words, and v1's floor of 4 characters
  without whitespace stays. So short options ("none", "true", "0") never hit. Counting on the option as written is
  the alternative reading; it would exclude, for example, "sqrt(2)". The owner decides (open question 5).
- **C1.** The adjudicator's labels are leak, illustrative and task_structure.
  - A rule that describes how the tasks are built, and how to act on it, without saying which answer is usually right
    is task_structure. Example: "when the options include a 'none of the above' style option and your computed value
    matches no option, choose that option". Such a rule is kept while `LEAKAGE_ALLOW_TASK_STRUCTURE = True`
    (protocol.py); with the switch off it is dropped as `leak_task_structure`.
  - A rule that encodes an answer distribution ("the last option is usually right", "prefer option B") is a leak.
  - Template answers are never a mechanical drop; the adjudicator sees them marked as template.
  - Seeds are not adjudicated (C4).
- **C-4, research part (P1).** A research rewrite of a rule made of parts revises only its action (`do`) and its
  alternative (`otherwise`). The model replies `{"do", "otherwise"?}`, and the program renders the text again with the
  recorded condition, so the condition the trigger was judged on stays the tested text's. The prompt states the
  constraints: the condition stays, do has at most 300 characters, otherwise at most 100, and the rule at most 600.
  The kept rule takes the new parts. A rule without parts takes v3's free-text rewrite. The statuses stay rewritten,
  kept and rewrite_failed.
- **P2 (adjudicator, forward checker).** The adjudicator's fixed prompt, and each forward-check prompt, end with one
  worked example on an imaginary colour task that matches no benchmark: a message and a valid reply.
  - Adjudicator: a colour-sorting task card, a rule with an incidental answer-word hit, and the reply "illustrative".
  - OfficeQA-style check: a two-row paint-tin table and the formula `41 - 17`.
  - LiveMath-style check: a passage on paint blends, `comb(6, 2)` and the verdict yes.

  The examples are part of the prompt text, and therefore of the roles' request hashes.
- **P4 (adjudicator).** As B6: three samples at temperature 0.7, and a tie is leak. Every vote's label and reason is
  kept with the decision.
- **P4 (forward checker).** Each forward item that passes the program's checks gets three sampled consistency-check
  calls at temperature 0.7.
  - Each sample replies `{"reason", "formula"}` (OfficeQA style) or `{"reason", "verdict"}` (LiveMath style).
  - A sample's outcome is agrees, `check_literal_not_in_table` or `check_mismatch` (OfficeQA), or agrees or
    `check_rejected` (LiveMath).
  - The outcome more than half of the samples give decides. Otherwise the item is dropped as `check_invalid`.
  - There are at most 45 check calls per (rule, target); v3 had 15.
  - Every sample's outcome and reason are kept in the round's item row.

## 10. Open questions (each with the choice the plan makes)

1. **One label per rule vs one label per hit (B4's wording "labels each hit").**
   - The plan makes one adjudication (3 votes) per rule text. It sees all the rule's hits, and its label is the
     rule's. The prompt says "a rule that leaks on any count is leak", so a leak hit drops the rule exactly as
     per-hit labels OR-ed together would.
   - Per-hit adjudication would cost 3 x (number of hits) calls per text. It would still need the whole-rule call for
     v3's unseen-condition policy and for rules without hits.
   - The owner may prefer per-hit labels, for example to keep a per-hit verdict in B3's record. The change is local:
     `adjudicate` would loop over `shown(hits)`.
   - **v2:** B4 is marked `needs_decision` on this point. The code is complete for the per-rule reading. Each
     adjudicated entry carries the rule's label (`adjudicated_label`), so B3's record shows the outcome on every hit.
     §8 states "one label per rule text, judged over all its hits".
2. **The tie rule.**
   - With three labels, a 1-1-1 split is a tie and is "leak", as the spec says. So is `[illustrative,
     task_structure, leak]`, although two votes would keep the rule. So is any sample set where invalid votes leave no
     majority.
   - Alternative: decide keep vs leak first (the majority of the two keep labels against leak), then the keep label.
     The plan follows the spec literally.
3. **The hard-drop definition.**
   - Characters are counted on the normalized answer, where punctuation becomes spaces: "Barack Obama" is exactly
     12 characters. A number's digits are counted with the point, and an option's content without whitespace.
   - "Not a dictionary word" is approximated by "not a single alphabetic token". So "constantinople" is never hard,
     while "ab12cd34ef56" and multi-word names are. A real word list would be new data, not a dependency.
   - Template answers are never hard.
   - **v2:** a number is hard only when the rule's written number (thousands separators removed) has at least 12
     characters too, so the rule must contain a 12-character answer, not merely an equal value. The template
     exemption, the digits-and-point counting and the dictionary approximation are listed in §8 as owner decisions.
     Each can be flipped by changing `_answer`'s `long`/`template` arguments in `hits`.
4. **The spec's "keep whatever stricter rule exists today for that case".** The plan reads it as keeping v1's
   matchers for the hard case: a whole-word run, a number by value whatever its separators, an option as a substring.
   It does not read it as keeping v3's mechanical drop of every non-word hit, which would contradict B4's "all hit
   kinds go to the adjudicator". This is documented in the module docstring.
5. **B12's counting.**
   - Words and characters are counted on the normalized option, where symbols split words. So "sqrt(2)" is 2 words
     and 5 squeezed characters and can hit, while "x + 1" (2 squeezed characters) and "none" never hit.
   - Counting on the option as written would exclude "sqrt(2)" (1 word, 7 characters).
   - **v2:** the reading is the owner's. §8's B12 sentence names both readings. The literal one would count words
     with a whitespace split of the option as written, keep the 4-character squeezed floor, and change one B12 test
     case.
6. **Token figures (§9, v2).** G3 requires at least 8000 tokens per sample for both the adjudicator and the forward
   checks: the literal maximum is 6,882 and about 7k. G4 owns `ROLE_TOKENS` and the caps. G4's newest worksheet
   already counts both at 8000 and fits its caps; G4.md as written (19:28) does not.
7. **C-4: the `when` stays fixed through the research rewrite and the self-test revision.**
   - **Research part: planned here, as G2's contract states it** (§4 (g), §4A, v2). The rewrite returns
     `{"do", "otherwise"?}`, and the code re-renders with the recorded `when`. v1's guard, which dropped a free-text
     rewrite that lost the opening, is replaced. It left an untold constraint (against B5) and stale `do`/`otherwise`.

     - It belongs to whoever takes the selftest's P1 format.
     - G3's `_admissible(check="revision")` works either way: `_parts` shows only what the text is made of.
8. **Kept rules with adjudicated hits and the forward step (v2: resolved by default).**
   - `step.selftest` calls `_forward` for similar candidates. It sends the rule text to the researcher's material
     requests and to the forward generator as its topic.
